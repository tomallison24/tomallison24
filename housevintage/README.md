# Home Vintage

[Home](../house/README.md) in a mid-century modern look, as its own app. It
does everything Home does - every device on the Signal dashboard in six views
(Favorites, Climate, Lights, Media, Security, Around the house), live over
Home Assistant's own WebSocket API, with a preview on sample readings until
it is connected. The devices and controls are described in
`house/README.md`; this page covers what differs.

## The look

Mid-century modern in the Retro colours, from the aOS Design Styles canvas:

- a cream ground and cards (walnut in dark mode), flat and round-cornered, in
  place of glass, skies, mist and motes;
- mustard, teal, burnt orange and olive, including the per-device accent
  colours (heat is orange, cooling teal, a full tank orange, a spare light
  mustard); the lights' colour swatches stay true colours;
- a mustard sun behind the title and three little bars under it;
- a walnut pill at the bottom for the view picker, with a mustard disc;
  outlined and solid-orange buttons; Futura, which iOS has built in, so no
  font is downloaded;
- the background is **Vintage** (cream or walnut) with nothing moving behind
  it. The other tones and drawings in Settings → Customization still work if
  you want them, but they were drawn for glass.

It is the last block of the style sheet in `index.html`; the glass styles
above it are still there underneath. Home Vintage is drawn this way for
everyone: the style picker in the design canvas is not built.

**The sky.** The page behind the title is Weather Vintage's: by day (Light) a cream
ground washed with mustard, with the sun and rays that turn very slowly; by night
(Dark) a walnut ground washed with deep teal, with the moon and twinkling stars.
It follows Light or Dark (the phone's, or the choice in aOS) and holds still with
reduced motion. `../home/sky.js` does it for every Vintage design and aOS (loaded in
`<head>`, listed in `sw.js`); this app only gives its disc its colour
(`--sky-disc`) and keeps its own sizes and places for the shapes, tuned to its
header. Its Vintage tone (Settings > Customization) is the sky's ground; any other tone you pick stays as picked. Checked by `aOS/scripts/sky-test.mjs`.

## What is separate from Home

It is a second copy of Home's page and scripts, not a setting, so the two can
change independently and Home is never touched. (Home is also drawn after the
Signal dashboard in `ha-config`; this app is a new look, so there is nothing to
change there.)

- **Own storage.** Every key is `housev.…` (Home's is `house.…`), so it has
  its own Home Assistant address and token, PIN, favorites, background and
  schedules, and its own offline cache (`housevintage-v2`). **Connect it once**
  the way you did Home: the family address fills itself in when you are signed
  in to aOS, and the token is a long-lived access token (see
  `house/README.md`, "Connecting to Home Assistant"). Making a second token
  named *AllisonOS Home Vintage* keeps the two easy to tell apart and to cut
  off separately.
- **Weather.** The weather pill reads Weather's saved place and units when
  Weather is used on the phone, exactly as Home does; it works without Weather.
- Everything else (the connection race, the control plumbing, the content
  security policy) is Home's, unchanged.

## Tests

`node housevintage/scripts/app-test.mjs` opens Home Vintage in its preview at
iPhone size, light and dark: it opens on Favorites, the lamps' night moon and
sun chips work, every view opens with something in it, and nothing errors or
trips the page's policy. `node housevintage/scripts/connect-test.mjs` checks
the connection race. Both run in CI (`apps-check.yml`).
`node housevintage/scripts/make-icons.mjs` redraws the icons from `icon.svg`
(a cream house on mustard with a teal sun).

The light-mode rules for Home's glass are generated by
`tools/lightgen.py` (between the `LIGHT-BEGIN`/`LIGHT-END` markers); the
Vintage block comes after them and does not need regenerating.
